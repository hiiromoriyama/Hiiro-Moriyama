This project focuses on the application of EXAFS to probe the local atomic structure and coordination environments in complex material systems. By utilizing high-intensity synchrotron radiation, we can resolve the short-range order that governs macroscopic chemical and physical properties.

Probing Local Atomic Environments

Extended X-Ray Absorption Fine Structure (EXAFS) is a premier spectroscopic technique used to determine the local geometric and electronic structure of matter. Unlike diffraction-based methods, EXAFS does not require long-range periodicity, making it an invaluable tool for studying amorphous materials, liquids, and complex molecular complexes.

The physical principle relies on the modulation of the X-ray absorption coefficient at energies just above the absorption edge of a specific element. These oscillations arise from the interference between the outgoing photoelectron wave and the wave backscattered by neighboring atoms.

Interactive Dynamics: Fe-H Lattice

Visualization of photoelectron scattering and structural distortion within the metallic lattice.

Through advanced data modeling and Fourier transform analysis, we can extract precise information regarding interatomic distances, coordination numbers, and the species of neighboring atoms. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of material behavior under various environmental conditions and structural transitions.
