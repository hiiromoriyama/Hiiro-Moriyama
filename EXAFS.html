EXAFS: Probing Local Environments

Extended X-Ray Absorption Fine Structure (EXAFS) is a premier spectroscopic technique used to determine the local geometric and electronic structure of matter.

Note: Explore how photoelectrons scatter after an iron atom’s K-edge is excited. Use the H-Conc slider to see real-time strain and the Thermal slider to simulate heat-induced vibrations (Debye-Waller effect).

HCP Lattice Dynamics: Zirconium Hydrides

Zirconium alloys are pivotal in nuclear cladding. The absorption of hydrogen leads to brittle hydride phases. Note the complex path distribution in the HCP structure.

Note (Zr-H): Purple spheres represent Zirconium in hexagonal layers. Hydrogen occupies the octahedral interstitial sites.

BCC Lattice Dynamics: Hydrogen in α-Iron

In BCC metals, Hydrogen atoms preferentially occupy tetrahedral interstitial sites, causing significant anisotropic expansion of the Iron lattice.

Note: Observe the physics-based distortion: insertion of Hydrogen causes a shift in the EXAFS oscillation frequency and phase.
