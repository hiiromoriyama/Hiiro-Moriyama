EXAFS: Probing Local Environments

Extended X-ray Absorption Fine Structure (EXAFS) provides a "fingerprint" of the local atomic structure. It is used to determine the distance, number, and species of neighbor atoms around a central absorbing atom.

HCP Lattice Dynamics (Zr-H)

Hexagonal Close-Packed (HCP) structures are critical in nuclear materials. This simulation shows the interstitial sites where hydrogen atoms reside, leading to hydride formation and potential embrittlement.

BCC Lattice Dynamics (Iron)

Modeling Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) iron lattices to observe thermal vibrations and atomic displacements under varying environmental conditions.

Tensile Test Lab: Hydrogen Embrittlement

This module simulates a tensile test on metal alloys. By adjusting the Hydrogen Content, you can observe how solute atoms interact with dislocations, reducing the plastic deformation range and inducing premature brittle fracture.

Core Physics: The engineering stress ($\sigma$) and strain ($\epsilon$) are calculated as: $$\sigma = \frac{P}{A_0} \quad , \quad \epsilon = \frac{L - L_0}{L_0}$$

User Guide: Use the slider in the simulation to increase hydrogen ppm. Note the reduction in the area under the curve (Toughness) as hydrogen concentration increases.
